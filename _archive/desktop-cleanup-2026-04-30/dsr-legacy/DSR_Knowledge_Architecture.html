2. Onboarding Interrogation

Questions that populate the artist's Operating Bible — tiered by user type

⚡ Design Principle:

Onboarding must feel like a conversation, not a form. The AI agent asks these questions in a chat interface. Each answer feeds the database and shapes the auto-generated Operating Bible. If OAuth is available (Spotify, Meta), pull data automatically and skip those questions.

Tier 1 Independent Artist

Goal: Build their profile, generate their Bible, give them a Command Center in under 5 minutes.

Identity (auto-pull where possible)

  1. What's your artist name?
  2. What genre do you make? (dropdown: bass music, dubstep, house, DnB, trap, experimental, other)
  3. What subgenre? (free text — this matters for ad targeting)
  4. Where are you based? (city, state)
  5. Are you signed to a label? If yes, which one?
  6. Do you have a manager? If yes, their name and email.

DSP & Distribution

  1. Link your Spotify for Artists (OAuth) — auto-pulls monthly listeners, popularity index, top cities
  2. Who is your distributor? (dropdown: DistroKid, TuneCore, VMG/Virgin, CD Baby, AWAL, United Masters, other)
  3. When was your last release? (date picker)
  4. How often do you release music? (dropdown: weekly, every 2 weeks, monthly, every 6-8 weeks, quarterly, irregularly)

Touring & Booking

  1. Have you played live shows? (yes/no — gates different onboarding paths)
  2. What's the lowest guarantee you'd accept for a show? (dollar amount — this becomes their floor)
  3. How many shows have you played in the last 12 months? (number)
  4. Do you have any upcoming shows? (if yes, intake form for each)

Goals (The Psychological Questions)

  1. What's your #1 goal for the next 6 months? (dropdown: grow streams, book more shows, get signed, build fanbase, make money, release an EP/album)
  2. What's your biggest pain point right now? (free text — this shapes AI agent personality and priorities)
  3. Are you optimizing for viral growth or touring longevity? (binary choice — fundamentally changes all advice)

Tier 2 Artist Manager

Goal: Set up their management company, add their roster, establish per-artist baselines.

Company Setup

  1. What's your management company name? (or just your name if solo)
  2. How many artists do you manage? (number — determines roster slots)
  3. What's your management commission rate? (percentage — typically 15-20%)
  4. Who handles booking — you, the artist, or a booking agent? (per artist)

Per-Artist Intake (repeats for each artist on roster)

  1. Run full Tier 1 onboarding for each artist
  2. What phase is this artist in? (dropdown: development, emerging, mid-level, established)
  3. What's this artist's priority this quarter? (free text)
  4. Are there any active contracts or deadlines for this artist? (free text)

Operations

  1. What's your total monthly marketing budget across all artists? (dollar amount)
  2. Who approves marketing spend — you or the artist? (per artist)
  3. Do you want the AI to auto-generate daily task lists for you? (yes/no)

Tier 3 Label Manager

Goal: Set up the label entity, configure roster permissions, establish release pipeline.

Label Setup

  1. Label name
  2. Genre focus (can be multiple)
  3. Distributor (label-wide default)
  4. How many artists currently on your roster? (number)
  5. Do your artists have their own management, or do you manage them? (this determines if they get Manager-tier features bundled)

Roster Configuration

  1. Add each artist — runs Tier 1 onboarding per artist
  2. Set permission level per artist: (dropdown: full visibility, limited visibility, no access)
  3. Does each artist get their own login to see their dashboard? (yes/no per artist)

Release Pipeline

  1. What's your standard release cycle? (e.g., 1 release every 6 weeks across roster)
  2. Do you accept outside demo submissions? (yes/no — gates A&R module)
  3. If yes: How many decision-makers vote on demos? (number)
  4. What's your voting rule? (e.g., 2 out of 3 must approve — DSR uses 2/3)
  5. What criteria do you score demos on? (DSR uses: Quality 40%, Reach 30%, Fit 30%)

Financials

  1. What's your label's monthly marketing budget? (dollar amount)
  2. How do you split ad spend across roster? (equal, weighted by release schedule, manual)
  3. Do you track master royalties through this platform? (yes/no — future feature flag)

🌳 Show Offer Decision Tree

When an artist or manager receives a show offer, the AI agent runs this logic:

INPUT: Offer amount, city, venue, date, promoter name ↓ STEP 1: Floor Check Is offer ≥ artist's minimum guarantee (from onboarding)? ├── NO → "This offer is below your floor of $[X]. Decline or counter at $[floor + 25%]." └── YES → Continue ↓ STEP 2: Market Check Has artist played this city before? ├── YES → What was the previous guarantee? │ ├── New offer < previous → "You're being offered less than last time ($[prev]). Counter at $[prev + 10%]." │ └── New offer ≥ previous → PASS └── NO → Flag as new market. "New market. Consider accepting at a lower rate to build the market, but not below your floor." ↓ STEP 3: Cost-Per-Ticket Analysis Estimate CPT = (ad spend + travel) / expected attendance ├── CPT > $5.00 → "This show will lose money at current ad rates. Need higher guarantee or promoter marketing commitment." ├── CPT $3.00-$5.00 → "Marginal. Profitable only if promoter covers marketing. Ask for marketing commitment in contract." └── CPT < $3.00 → "Good deal. Profitable show." ↓ STEP 4: Calendar Check Is there another show within 3 days / 300 miles? ├── YES → "Route opportunity. Try to book both shows on the same run to split travel costs." └── NO → Check if travel cost makes this a standalone fly date ├── Standalone fly date + offer < $2,000 → "Standalone fly date under $2K. Ask for travel buyout or hotel." └── Otherwise → PASS ↓ STEP 5: Promoter Check Is this promoter in the database? ├── YES → Pull promoter grade (A/B/C/D/F) │ ├── Grade D or F → "This promoter has a poor track record. Require 100% deposit upfront." │ └── Grade A-C → PASS └── NO → "Unknown promoter. Require 50% deposit. Google them before signing." ↓ FINAL OUTPUT: ACCEPT / COUNTER (with specific number and terms) / DECLINE (with reason)

📝 Promoter Grading System

Every promoter interaction is tracked. Grade is auto-calculated after each show.

GradeCriteriaAgent Behavior
APaid on time, marketed well, venue was good, attendance met expectations, professional communicationAuto-approve future offers from this promoter
BMostly good, minor issues (slight delay on payment, mediocre marketing)Accept but flag any previous issues in contract
CMixed — some shows good, some had issues. Inconsistent.Require 50% deposit, add marketing commitment to contract

Post-Show Grading Questions (auto-prompted after show date)

  1. Did the promoter pay the full guarantee? (yes / partial / no)
  2. Was payment on time? (yes / late — how late?)
  3. How was the promoter's marketing effort? (1-5 scale)
  4. What was approximate attendance vs capacity? (percentage)
  5. Any issues? (free text — stored for future reference)

💵 Counter-Offer Formula

When the AI recommends a counter, it uses this formula:

Counter Amount = MAX of: 1. Artist's minimum floor + 25% 2. Previous guarantee in same market + 10% 3. Offer amount + (estimated travel cost / 2) 4. CPT-adjusted minimum: (target CPT × expected attendance) + ad budget Always ask for: - 50% deposit (100% for C/D grade promoters or first-time) - Green room with water/snacks minimum - Sound check time specified in contract - Marketing commitment clause (promoter spends $X minimum) Ask for when applicable: - Hotel buyout (standalone fly dates or 3+ hour drives) - Travel buyout (flights over $300) - Meal buyout ($25-50/person) - Plus-one guest list spots

4. DSP Algorithmic Playbook

Platform-by-platform hack framework — templatized for any release

⏰ CRITICAL TIMING:

The first 7 days after release determine permanent algorithmic placement across all platforms. The AI agent must trigger the release checklist automatically on release day and track completion of each action.

🟢 Spotify — Release Week Actions

  1. Discovery Mode: Toggle ON immediately on release day. Trades ~30% royalty rate for 2-3x algorithmic impressions. Worth it for the first 2-4 weeks of every release.
  2. Canvas: Upload a 3-8 second looping video. Tracks with Canvas get 145% more shares. Must be uploaded within 24 hours of release.
  3. Clips: Record 30-60 second commentary clip. Appears in Spotify feed. Personal connection increases save rate.
  4. Save Campaign (Meta Ads): Run $50-100 over 7 days targeting genre fans in tour markets. Direct to Spotify pre-save/save link. Target: push save-to-stream ratio above 10%.
  5. Pitch to Editorial: Must be done 7+ days BEFORE release via Spotify for Artists. Include genre, mood, instruments, and upcoming tour dates.
  6. Playlist Seeding: Submit to independent playlist curators via SubmitHub or direct outreach. Target 5-10 playlists in genre.

Key Metric: Save-to-Stream Ratio

If saves/streams > 10%, track triggers Discover Weekly and Release Radar placement. The AI agent should monitor this daily during week 1 and alert if ratio drops below target.

🔴 Apple Music — Shazam Spike Strategy

Shazam is Apple Music's #1 external signal. A regional spike in Shazams triggers "Trending on Shazam" which feeds Apple's editorial algorithm.

  1. Geo-Targeted Meta Ads: Run $50-75 in ads targeting a SINGLE city (ideally next tour stop) 3-5 days before the show. The ad plays a clip of the track. Users Shazam it. Concentrated geo = trending alert.
  2. Artist Message: Record a voice note through Apple Music for Artists. Goes to all followers. Do this within 48 hours of release.
  3. Lyrics Sync: Confirm lyrics are correct in Musixmatch. Apple uses this for voice search matching ("Hey Siri, play that song that goes...")

📱 TikTok — Audio Virality Framework

  1. Post 2-3 TikToks with SEO-optimized titles on release day. Titles matter more than hashtags. Example: "heavy bass drop 2026 dubstep" or "filthiest drop you'll hear today"
  2. Make audio available as a Sound — this is the #1 signal. When other users use your audio, TikTok's algorithm treats it as a trending sound.
  3. Ask support artists / friends to post using the audio. 5-10 videos using the same sound in week 1 can trigger trending.
  4. Engage with comments aggressively in the first 2 hours after posting. TikTok's algorithm weighs early engagement heavily.

5. Touring Phase System

Every show moves through 4 phases. The AI agent tracks phase and triggers actions automatically.

Phase Definitions & Triggers

PhaseTimelineTriggerAI Agent Actions
ANNOUNCEMENT 4-8 weeks before show Show confirmed, contract signed Generate announcement content (3-5 posts). Launch Shazam Spike if within budget. Add to touring grid. Draft "save the date" social post.
ON-SALE 3-5 weeks before show Tickets go live Generate ticket push content. Track ticket velocity if data available. Launch retargeting ads if pixel is set up. Monitor promoter marketing effort.
FINAL PUSH 7-10 days before show Auto-triggered by date Generate urgency content ("7 DAYS", "THIS FRIDAY"). Increase ad spend. Daily social posts. Push ticket link in every post. Direct message outreach to fans in that city.
MAINTENANCE Post-announcement, low activity Show is confirmed but >4 weeks out Periodic reminder posts (1-2/week). Keep show in content rotation. No heavy ad spend. Focus marketing on closer shows.

🔄 Phase Transition Rules

The AI agent auto-transitions shows between phases based on date proximity:

Show date minus current date: ├── > 28 days → ANNOUNCEMENT or MAINTENANCE ├── 21-28 days → ON-SALE (if tickets are live) ├── 7-10 days → FINAL PUSH (auto-trigger urgency content) ├── 0-6 days → FINAL PUSH — MAXIMUM URGENCY └── Past date → POST-SHOW → Trigger promoter grading questions

✏️ Caption Generation Rules

When the AI agent generates captions, it must follow these rules:

  • Voice: Match the artist's tone. Bass music artists are not corporate. Keep it raw, direct, hyped. Avoid marketing-speak.
  • Length: Instagram captions: 1-3 sentences max. Twitter: 1 sentence + link. TikTok: SEO-optimized title, not a caption.
  • CTA: Every post needs ONE clear call-to-action. Not three. Either "grab tickets," "save the track," or "tag your crew." Pick one.
  • Urgency words (Final Push phase): "THIS FRIDAY," "LAST CALL," "almost sold out," "don't sleep," "link in bio NOW"
  • Avoid: "We're so excited to announce..." (too corporate). "Don't miss out on this incredible..." (too salesy). Generic emojis without context.
  • Include: City name in every show post. Venue name when confirmed. Ticket link or "link in bio." Tag venue and promoter.

7. Release Cadence & Decay Prevention

The math behind staying alive in the algorithm

🚨 THE DECAY RULE:

No new ISRC every 6-8 weeks = Spotify Artist Quality Score decays. The AI agent must track the last release date and auto-alert at the 4-week mark that a new release needs to be locked.

📆 Release Cycle Timeline

For every release, the AI agent tracks this timeline automatically:

🔄 Waterfall Strategy

Release singles individually, then bundle them into an EP/album using the SAME ISRCs under a new UPC. The EP inherits all accumulated Track Quality Scores from the individual singles.

Example Waterfall: Single 1 (Week 0) → ISRC: US-XX1-26-00001 → Accumulates streams/saves Single 2 (Week 6) → ISRC: US-XX1-26-00002 → Accumulates streams/saves Single 3 (Week 12) → ISRC: US-XX1-26-00003 → Accumulates streams/saves Single 4 (Week 18) → ISRC: US-XX1-26-00004 → Accumulates streams/saves ↓ EP Release (Week 20) → New UPC, same 4 ISRCs + 1-2 bonus tracks → EP inherits ALL stream counts and quality scores from singles → Appears as "new release" in algorithm while carrying old momentum

Minimum Release Schedule (2026)

7 releases needed (one every ~6 weeks through December). The AI agent should maintain a rolling release calendar and alert when the pipeline is running dry.

8. A&R Demo Intake System

DirtySnatcha Records ONLY — this is NOT a universal feature

⚠️ SCOPE RESTRICTION:

The 2/3 voting system is SPECIFIC to DirtySnatcha Records' demo submission process. It does NOT apply to booking decisions, marketing budgets, release scheduling, or any other platform feature. Other labels using the platform can configure their own A&R process with different rules during onboarding.

📥 DSR Demo Submission Pipeline

Step 1: Submission Intake

Artist submits demo via form (public-facing on DSR website or in-platform). Required fields:

  • Artist name
  • Track title
  • Private SoundCloud or Google Drive link (streaming link, NOT download)
  • Genre/subgenre
  • Social links (minimum: Spotify + 1 social)
  • Brief description of the track (optional but weighted)

Step 2: Auto-Scoring (AI Pre-Filter)

Before human review, the AI agent scores the submission on three criteria:

CriteriaWeightWhat It MeasuresData Source
Quality40%Production value, mixdown, mastering, originalityHuman review (cannot be auto-scored)
Reach30%Spotify monthly listeners, social following, engagement rateAuto-pulled from Spotify API + social links
Fit30%Does this sound belong on DirtySnatcha Records? Genre alignment, brand fitHuman review

The AI can auto-score Reach by pulling Spotify data. Quality and Fit require human ears.

Step 3: The 2/3 Vote

DSR has 3 decision-makers (configurable in label settings). Each listens to the demo and votes:

  • YES — I want this on the label
  • NO — Pass
  • MAYBE — Needs discussion (counts as abstain, not a vote)

Rule: 2 out of 3 YES votes = demo accepted. 2 out of 3 NO votes = demo rejected. Any other combination = discussion needed.

Step 4: Response

  • Accepted: AI drafts acceptance email with next steps (contract, release timeline, asset requests)
  • Rejected: AI drafts polite rejection with optional feedback (if label chooses to give feedback)
  • Discussion: Flag for team meeting. AI summarizes each voter's notes.

⚙️ Configurable for Other Labels

When a different label signs up for Tier 3, the onboarding asks them to configure their own A&R process. Options include:

  • Number of decision-makers (1-5)
  • Voting threshold (majority, unanimous, or custom)
  • Scoring criteria and weights (fully customizable)
  • Whether to accept outside demos at all
  • Auto-reject threshold (e.g., if Reach score < 20, auto-reject without human review)

10. AI Agent Behavior Rules

How the Gemini agent should behave when talking to users

🧠 Agent Persona

The AI agent is NOT a generic chatbot. It is an Elite Music Industry Manager. It speaks with authority, gives direct advice, and doesn't hedge. Here are the behavior rules:

  • Be direct. Don't say "You might want to consider..." Say "Do this." Artists need direction, not options paralysis.
  • Be specific. Don't say "You should promote your show." Say "Post a countdown TikTok with the track audio at 6pm today. Caption: 'Tampa in 5 days. Who's pulling up?' Tag the venue."
  • Use their data. Every recommendation must reference the artist's actual numbers. "Your save ratio is at 8%. You need 10% to trigger Discover Weekly. Push the save campaign harder today."
  • Don't sugarcoat. If a show is going to lose money, say so. "This offer is $500 below your floor. The CPT on this market is $6.20. Decline or counter at $2,800."
  • Prioritize ruthlessly. When giving daily marching orders, rank by urgency and impact. Don't list 20 tasks. Give them the 3 things that matter today.
  • Know the music industry. Reference DSP algorithms, promoter dynamics, contract terms, and touring logistics naturally. Don't explain basics unless asked.
  • Adapt tone to user tier. Label managers want data and efficiency. Independent artists need more encouragement and education. Match accordingly.

🚫 Agent Guardrails

Things the AI agent must NEVER do:

  • Never give legal advice. If a user asks about contract terms, say "I can flag the key terms, but you need a music attorney to review this." Recommend entertainment lawyers.
  • Never guarantee outcomes. Don't say "This will go viral" or "You'll sell out." Say "Based on your data, sellout probability is high" or "This strategy has the best chance of triggering algorithmic placement."
  • Never access another user's data. Even if a manager asks about a competitor artist, the agent only knows what's in their database. Publicly available data (Spotify monthly listeners) is fine to reference.
  • Never auto-execute financial transactions. The agent can recommend ad spend, draft campaigns, and build specs — but the user must click "approve" before any money is spent.
  • Never share label financials with artist-tier users. If a label has an artist on their roster who also has their own Tier 1 login, the artist cannot see the label's financial data, other artists' guarantees, or internal label communications. Row Level Security enforces this.
