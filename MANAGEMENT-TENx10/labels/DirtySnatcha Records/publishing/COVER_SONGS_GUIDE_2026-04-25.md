# Cover Songs — What to Do

**Date:** 2026-04-25  
**Context:** DirtySnatcha has several cover recordings in his catalog. These require different treatment than originals across every royalty system. This document covers what applies to each one.

---

## The Core Rule

You own the **master recording**. You do NOT own the **composition**. Every royalty system treats these separately.

| Channel | Your role on an original | Your role on a cover |
|---|---|---|
| BMI/ASCAP (performance) | Writer + Publisher | Nothing — original writers collect |
| MLC (mechanical, streaming) | Publisher | Nothing — original publisher collects |
| CMRRA (Canadian mechanical) | Publisher | Nothing — original publisher collects |
| SoundExchange (digital performance) | Rights Owner + Featured Artist | Rights Owner + Featured Artist ✓ |
| Mechanical license (to distribute legally) | Not required | Required |

## Mechanical License — How It Works for Covers

For US streaming of cover songs, the distributor (VMG/Label Engine) is typically responsible for obtaining a statutory mechanical license. The MLC processes these payments — but the money goes to the **original publisher**, not you.

**If your distributor did NOT license it:**
1. Contact VMG: "Did you obtain mechanical licenses for [Genie In a Bottle / Gangsta's Paradise / Everybody]?"
2. If not: use **Songfile** (Harry Fox Agency) for covers that are straightforward lyrics/melody covers
3. For covers that SAMPLE other recordings (like Gangsta's Paradise): Songfile does not help with the master use or sample clearance — those require direct negotiation with the master owner and publisher
