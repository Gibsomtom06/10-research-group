# Contract audit — DirtySnatcha Records
**2026-07-10.** Every contract on disk, in Google Drive (thomas@ and demos@) and in Gmail.

---

## The headline

**Coverage: 49 of 129 DSR releases have a contract on file (38%).**

That is the optimistic number. The real one is worse:

> **Of 28 demos@-era contracts read end to end, exactly TWO are executed by both the
> artist and the label.** Twenty-two are not. Four are scans that could not be read.

A filled-in contract nobody signed is not a contract.

## The finding that changes the publishing plan

**Not one demos@-era contract assigns the publisher's share.**

Every readable one uses the old template:

> *"This agreement confirms that the artist(s) named below is/are the sole writer(s)
> for this composition and hereby agree to the following publisher's divisions."*
> *"Ownership of the said recording below: 50/50 split ownership between the label
> and artist/s."*
> Term: **Lifetime.** Territory: **Worldwide.** Paid via automatic Symphonic split pay.

Compare the current (2024+) template, which does assign it:

> *"The Artist hereby irrevocably assigns, conveys, and sets over to the Label's
> publishing designee ('Publisher') **100% ownership**… The Artist retains 100% of
> the Writer's Share."*

So the label's publishing claim is **era-dependent**:

| era | master | publisher's share |
|---|---|---|
| demos@ template (~2022–2023) | "50/50 split ownership" — ambiguous | **never assigned** |
| current template (2024+) | label owns; artist gets 50% of Net Receipts | **100% assigned to LAB10 / DSR Publishing** |

**Do not file a publisher's-share claim on a work whose contract never granted one,
and never signed.** That is most of the 2022–2023 catalogue.

The old template is also internally weak: "50/50 split ownership" of a recording says
nothing about who administers, and it calls DSR the "Publishing Company" while
granting no publishing rights.

## A third entity name

Every old contract prints:

> **Publishing Company: DirtySnatcha Records / DIRTYSNATCHA LLC**
> Address: 2439 Matilda Court, Warren, MI, 48092, USA

`DIRTYSNATCHA LLC` — not "DirtySnatcha Records LLC". Dated 2022–2023, i.e. **before
the LLC Thomas remembers forming (~2023/24)**. This bears directly on the 89 masters
carrying `℗ DirtySnatcha Records` with a pre-2023 year. Which entity actually existed,
and when, is now a question for the filing paperwork, not for me.

The contracts also print `demos@dirtysnatcha.com` as the label's contact, while the
Drive files are owned by `demos@dirtysnatcharecords.com`.

## Why nobody could find these contracts

`demos@` files them **by track title, with no contract word at all**:

    Bad Vibes.pdf        Torcha - Scorpion.pdf       DENNETT - FOG HORN.pdf
    Nightmare Agreement.pdf                          RIPTIDE DV & CH SIGNED.pdf

Searching Drive for "contract", "DSR" or "agreement" returns none of the first three.
The only reliable method is to search **by every track title in the catalogue**, which
is how the found set roughly tripled.

`demos@` is a **demo inbox**, not a contract vault: it receives submissions (HVRCRFT,
The Wahala Tribe). Contracts go out from `thomas@` with the subject *"(Approved by DSR)"*.

## What to do, in order

1. **Stop.** File nothing with ASCAP/BMI/MLC for the publisher's share on any
   2022–2023 release until its contract is found, read, and confirmed to grant it.
   (Also blocked by Songtrust — see `project_songtrust_replacement`.)
2. **Countersign.** Five contracts have the artist's signature and no label signature.
   That is the cheapest fix in this document. Get Leigh to sign them.
3. **Re-paper the blanks.** Sixteen releases have an unsigned draft. Send the current
   template — which *does* assign the publisher's share — and get both signatures.
4. **Read the four scans.** `EXCLUSIVE AGREEMENT.pdf` (2.4 MB) doesn't even name its
   artist. BBX is a photo of a page.
5. **Adobe Sign.** Two accounts hold executed copies. `KAIME Of The Sea EP` and
   `Monstercat & WHOiSEE — GREEN ONIONS` were signed there and exist nowhere else.
6. **Resolve the entity.** `DIRTYSNATCHA LLC` vs `DirtySnatcha Records LLC`, and when
   each existed.
