# Live Performance Royalty Recovery — Product Brief
*10 Research Group · April 2026 · Confidential*

---

## The Problem

PROs (BMI, ASCAP) collect blanket performance licenses from every venue, festival, and streaming platform. That money only flows to writers when performers submit setlists. Electronic and DJ artists almost never submit them — their sets are fluid, undocumented, and mixed. The result: independent producers whose tracks appear in major DJ sets collect zero on those performances, indefinitely.

YouTube holds millions of documented live performances. Liquid Stranger's Red Rocks set is publicly archived. He played a DirtySnatcha + CARBIN track. No claim was ever filed. That money is sitting at BMI right now.

This is not an edge case. It is the standard operating condition for every independent bass music producer.

---

## The Solution

An automated pipeline that:

1. Scans YouTube live sets using audio fingerprinting (ACRCloud / AudD API)
2. Cross-references matches against a registered catalog
3. Generates PRO-ready documentation (title, ISWC, writer, publisher, venue, date, estimated duration)
4. Queues and submits BMI/ASCAP performance documentation claims — no performer cooperation required

A publisher or writer can file a performance claim at BMI for any documented performance of their composition. We own that right. We do not need Liquid Stranger's team to answer an email.

---

## How We're Different

**Nobody is doing this systematically.** Pex, Tunesat, and Soundmouse address content ID and sync licensing — not PRO live performance gaps. The PROs themselves have no automated ingestion from YouTube evidence. This gap exists because it sits between two systems that don't talk to each other.

We are the artists. We test on our own catalog first, prove recovery, then license the pipeline to other labels and publishers. No partnership needed to launch. The proof of concept is a single YouTube video and one BMI claim.

---

## What We're Solving

- **$500M+ in live performance royalties uncollected annually** (conservative industry estimate)
- **No tooling exists** for retroactive YouTube-to-PRO documentation at scale
- **Independent producers bear the entire cost** of a system that was designed for acts with label infrastructure
